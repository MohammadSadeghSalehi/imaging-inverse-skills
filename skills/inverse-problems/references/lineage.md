## What to take from each source

Schönlieb. Treat a learned reconstructor as a regulariser or an unrolled variational model, and demand a stability story. The survey to organise the field is Arridge, Maass, Öktem, Schönlieb, "Solving inverse problems using data-driven models", Acta Numerica, 2019, doi:10.1017/S0962492919000059. Adversarial regularisers (Lunz, Öktem, Schönlieb, ICML 2018) put a Lipschitz network inside the variational objective as the penalty, which is a different object from a GAN that emits the image. Weakly convex regularisers still admit a convergent primal-dual method (Shumaylov, Budd, Mukherjee, Schönlieb, ICML 2024). Bilevel learning is how a variational model gets its parameters from data ("Bilevel parameter learning for higher-order total variation regularisation models" on her profile).

Ehrhardt. The forward operator is often a sum of blocks, the noise is often Poisson, and a second modality is information. Stochastic PDHG is the solver for subset-friendly convex imaging problems (Chambolle, Ehrhardt, Richtárik, Schönlieb, SIAM Journal on Optimization, 2018). Adaptive step sizes are the follow-up (Chambolle, Delplancke, Ehrhardt, Schönlieb, Tang, Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision, 2024, doi:10.1007/s10851-024-01174-1). Parallel level sets are the structural prior when an anatomical image should share edge direction with the target ("Vector-valued image processing by parallel level sets", "Joint reconstruction of PET-MRI by exploiting structural similarity", "PET Reconstruction with an Anatomical MRI Prior using Parallel Level Sets"). Sampling patterns are parameters of the imaging system and can be learned ("Learning the sampling pattern for MRI"). Equivariance belongs in the reconstructor when the physics commutes with a group ("Equivariant neural networks for inverse problems", "Imaging With Equivariant Deep Learning").

Pock. Write the objective, then the saddle-point form, then a primal-dual iteration with an explicit step-size condition. The algorithm is Chambolle and Pock, "A first-order primal-dual algorithm for convex problems with applications to imaging", Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision, 2011. The textbook account is Chambolle and Pock, "An introduction to continuous optimization for imaging", Acta Numerica, 2016. Total generalised variation removes TV staircasing (Bredies, Kunisch, Pock, "Total generalized variation"). Learned reconstruction in this line is a variational network or a trainable reaction-diffusion, not an unconstrained U-Net: Hammernik, Klatzer, Kobler, Recht, Sodickson, Pock, "Learning a variational network for reconstruction of accelerated MRI data", Magnetic Resonance in Medicine, 2018; Chen and Pock, "Trainable nonlinear reaction diffusion". Nonconvex inertial proximal steps are iPiano (Ochs, Chen, Brox, Pock). Diagonal preconditioners are how anisotropic operators get legal step sizes ("Diagonal preconditioning for first order primal-dual algorithms in convex optimization").

Neumayer. A network is allowed to replace a proximal map or a regulariser only when the replacement keeps the property the proof uses. Convex-ridge regularisers: Goujon, Neumayer, Bohra, Ducotterd, Unser, "A Neural-Network-Based Convex Regularizer for Inverse Problems", IEEE Transactions on Computational Imaging, 2023, doi:10.1109/TCI.2023.3306100. Weakly convex regularisers with a prescribed modulus, so the denoising energy stays convex and the inverse-problem iteration still converges: Goujon, Neumayer, Unser, SIAM Journal on Imaging Sciences, 2024, doi:10.1137/23M1565243. Proximal networks: Hasannasab, Hertrich, Neumayer, Plonka, Setzer, Steidl, "Parseval proximal neural networks", Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications, 2020; Hertrich, Neumayer, Steidl, "Convolutional proximal neural networks and plug-and-play algorithms", Linear Algebra and its Applications, 2021. Those networks are averaged by construction, so plug-and-play forward-backward has a convergence argument. Stability of the reconstruction map is part of the result, not a post-hoc plot ("Stability of image-reconstruction algorithms").

Chambolle. Total variation is minimised from the dual ball, and accelerated proximal methods need a separate argument for iterate convergence. Chambolle, "An algorithm for total variation minimization and applications", Journal of Mathematical Imaging and Vision, 2004, is the projected-gradient method on the dual; the step bound sits in the imaging-optimisation algorithms reference. Chambolle and Pock 2011 is the general saddle point. "On the convergence of the iterates of the fast iterative shrinkage/thresholding algorithm": the original FISTA momentum gives a rate on function values; iterate convergence wants the Chambolle–Dossal momentum. Mumford–Shah and convex relaxations of partitions are the segmentation functionals on his profile; use them when the unknown is a partition, and hand the algorithm back to imaging-optimisation.

DeepInverse, ASTRA, CIL, and LION. Which package owns the projector and the solver is `libraries.md`. DeepInverse remains the general imaging library: operators, data fidelities, priors, classical solvers, unfolded solvers, self-supervised losses, and sampling. API names in `deepinv.md` are from the 0.4.2 documentation; check the installed package before writing code.

Plug-and-play, RED, diffusion posterior sampling, Pereyra's Langevin methods, Tachella's equivariant imaging, the Pesquet, Repetti, and Chouzenoux lines (maximally monotone plug-and-play, unfolded proximal networks, hypothesis tests for uncertainty), Peyré and Poon (optimal transport, off-the-grid sparse recovery, parameter selection by SURE), Korolev (imperfect and learned forward operators), multilevel Monte Carlo, Benning's regularisation survey, and the French and American lines (Kamilov, Bouman, Milanfar, Unser, Hurault, Lustig, Candès) are in `communities.md`. Condat–Vũ, variable-metric and multilevel optimisation, and structure identification are in the imaging-optimisation algorithms reference. Do not restate them here.
